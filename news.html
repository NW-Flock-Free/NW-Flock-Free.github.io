Wrongful stops and arrests

  • Institute for Justice: dozens pulled over, held at gunpoint, or jailed over camera errors Includes a gunpoint stop and a police-dog attack after a "7" was misread as a "2."
  • ACLU: San Francisco woman pulled from her car at gunpoint over a misread plate
  • Denver7: Aurora police detain a Black family after a plate-reader error
  • Times of San Diego: a Flock reader linked a San Diego man to a violent crime; he was five miles away He spent nearly a month in jail before the charges were dropped.

Officer misuse

  • KWCH: Kansas police lieutenant arrested, accused of using a Flock database to stalk his estranged wife
  • Wisconsin Examiner: Wisconsin communities grapple with police misuse of Flock surveillance A running account of the Wisconsin cases.
  • WTMJ: a second Milwaukee detective charged with misusing the Flock system July 8, 2026. Twenty unauthorized searches, plus a GPS tracker; he claimed it was for training.
  • FOX 11: Menasha officer accused of using Flock off duty to track a woman's car Charged Jan. 2026 with misconduct in public office; bound over for trial. Charged is not convicted.
  • The Marshall Project: how police cameras are open to an officer's abuse
  • Atlanta Journal-Constitution: more Georgia officers arrested over Flock camera misuse July 2026. Five Albany officers were arrested and fired, and a Greene County deputy was arrested after an audit flagged her searches. It surfaced through department audits, not complaints. Charged is not convicted.
  • Institute for Justice: officers have been accused of using plate readers to track romantic interests at least 28 times Count updated July 25, 2026. IJ says nearly all of those officers were criminally charged and left their departments. Three of the cases it describes are in Wisconsin.

Shared with ICE and used against abortion seekers

  • NPR: cities cancel Flock contracts over immigration-surveillance concerns
  • UW Center for Human Rights: Flock exposes Washington data to immigration enforcement
  • EFF/ACLU: demand police stop sharing data with ICE and anti-abortion states Documents the Texas search for a woman who ended a pregnancy.
  • The Source (Bend, OR): federal immigration officials made 279 queries into Bend's Flock data in three weeks
  • Dayton Daily News: Dayton suspends Flock after finding 7,000-plus immigration-related searches

Data sharing under legal challenge (lawsuits and AG actions)

  • Class action: Flock shared California plate data with federal agencies 1.6 million times
  • FOX 11 LA: LAPD sued over Flock cameras
  • EFF: the Supreme Court says the Constitution protects your location data June 29, 2026, in Chatrie v. United States. The case is about geofence warrants for phone data, not cameras, and EFF does not say it covers plate readers. Our read is that its logic, that bulk location tracking is a search, could reach them as future cases test it.

Mission creep and breaches

  • EFF: readers used for school-residency checks, background checks, and noise complaints
  • Central Current: Syracuse exposed driver data, searched 4.4 million times in a year
  • EFF: new vulnerabilities prove mass surveillance is itself a public-safety threat
  • WRAL: North Carolina's new budget opens every state road to license-plate cameras July 17, 2026. WRAL reviewed the 32 agencies in the state's pilot and found 30 had no policy on surveilling protests or protected classes. Only Asheville and Raleigh did.

The camera company's own credibility

  • ACLU: Flock has repeatedly misled city councils and the public

The movement is winning

  • State of Surveillance: dozens of cities have canceled contracts, and the list keeps growing
  • Truthout: the nationwide resistance is gaining steam
  • Bolts: Verona, Wisconsin ended its Flock contract
  • Wisconsin Watch: Flock loses another Wisconsin contract as the backlash spreads
  • Cap Times: UW-Madison police let their Flock contract expire and are removing the cameras July 2026. The contract expired June 26 and the equipment is being pulled. In fairness, the department says it is looking for a different plate-reader vendor rather than dropping the technology, and will seek campus input first.
  • WDIV Detroit: Westland, Michigan is removing its 10 Flock cameras July 2026. The police chief did not bring a renewal to the city council after residents raised privacy concerns and questions about who else can search the data. The contract expires at the end of July.
  • WBAY: Appleton City Council votes unanimously to end its Flock contract July 15, 2026. The city stopped using the cameras on June 30 and covered them up.

Right here at home

  • Fox21: Ashland Police Department to remove Flock cameras from the city Our local proof it can be beaten.

Sources include the ACLU, EFF, the Institute for Justice, and local and national news outlets. Dates vary; follow each link for the full story.
